# Hr-Attrition-Dashboard
## Project Summary
Performed end-to-end HR attrition analysis using Power BI to identify employee turnover patterns, high-risk workforce segments, and key factors associated with employee attrition. Analyzed 1,470 employees and 237 employee exits, resulting in an overall attrition rate of 16.1%, and built an interactive dashboard to support data-driven employee retention and workforce planning decisions.

## Problem Statement
- What is the overall employee attrition rate?
- Which departments have the highest attrition?
- Which salary bands have higher attrition rates?
- How does attrition vary across different age groups?
- How does employee attrition differ by gender?
- Does overtime have an association with higher employee attrition?
- How does business travel frequency relate to employee attrition?
- How does marital status vary across attrition rates?
- Which employee segments should HR prioritize for retention initiatives?

## Dataset used
- <a href="https://github.com/ShivamAnalyst/Hr-Attrition-Dashboard/blob/main/HR-Employee-Attrition.csv">Hr Attrition Data</a>

## Tools and Technologies Used
- Microsoft Power BI – Interactive dashboard development, KPI cards, charts, tables, slicers, and business-focused visualizations.
- Power Query – Data cleaning, transformation, column standardization, and preparation of HR data for analysis.
- DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) – Created measures and calculated metrics for headcount, employee exits, and attrition rate.
- Data Visualization & Analytics Techniques – Attrition trend analysis, workforce segmentation, comparative analysis, and KPI-driven reporting.

## Key Insights
- The organization has 1,470 employees with 237 employee exits, resulting in an overall 16.1% attrition rate.
- Sales has the highest departmental attrition rate at 21%, followed by Human Resources at 19%, indicating higher turnover within these departments.
- Sales Representatives have the highest job-role attrition rate at 39.8%, followed by Laboratory Technicians at 23.9% and Human Resources roles at 23.1%.
- Employees in the Under 3K salary band have the highest attrition rate at 28.6%, compared with 8.9% for employees earning Above 10K.
- Employees aged 18–25 show the highest age-group attrition rate at 35.8%, followed by the 26–35 age group at 19.1%.
- Employees working Overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate of 30.5%, compared with 10.4% for employees without overtime.
- Employees with frequent business travel have a 24.9% attrition rate, compared with 8.0% among non-travel employees, indicating a strong association between frequent travel and employee turnover.
- Single employees show a higher attrition rate of 25.5%, compared with 12.5% for married and 10.1% for divorced employees.
- Attrition is higher among male employees (17.0%) compared with female employees (14.8%).
- The analysis highlights younger employees, lower salary bands, overtime employees, frequent travelers, and specific job roles as high-attrition segments that may require greater attention from HR.

## Business Impact & Recommendations
- Recommended prioritizing employee retention initiatives within high-attrition departments and job roles, particularly Sales and Sales Representatives.
- Recommended reviewing compensation and career-growth opportunities for employees in lower salary bands, where attrition is considerably higher.
- Recommended evaluating overtime patterns and workload distribution to identify potential employee burnout and retention risks.
- Recommended developing targeted retention strategies for younger employees, particularly employees in the 18–25 age group.
- Recommended reviewing the impact of frequent business travel and exploring workload or travel-management strategies for frequently traveling employees.
- The dashboard enables HR stakeholders to identify high-risk employee segments, monitor attrition KPIs, and support data-driven workforce planning and employee retention decisions.
